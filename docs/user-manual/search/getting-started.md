# Getting Started with the Search Bar

The search bar sits at the top of every screen in Edamame, so you can look something up without
leaving what you're doing. It searches your clients, your cases and your tasks at the same time.

## What it searches

A single query is matched against all of these at once:

- Client names
- The client attached to a case
- The applicant on a case (which can be a different person to the client — for example a dependent
  applicant)
- The client related to a task
- Case titles and case numbers
- Task titles

## Searching by name and visa together

The search bar understands visa vocabulary, so you can combine a person's name with the kind of
visa you're thinking of and it will narrow down to the right file. For example:

- `John Doe 186` — John Doe's subclass 186 case
- `John Doe work visa` — John Doe's work-sponsored (subclass 482) case
- `Smith partner visa` — the Smiths' partner (820/801) case
- `Chen skilled` — Chen's skilled visa case (189/190/491)

You can type the subclass number directly (`482`, `190`, `500`) or use everyday wording like
"work visa", "employer sponsored", "PR", "partner visa", "student", "graduate", "working holiday"
or "state nominated".

Names don't have to be exact — partial names and small typos still find the right person, so
`Jhon Doe` or just `Doe` will do.

If nothing matches a person, the search falls back to looking directly at case numbers, case
titles and task titles — so you can paste in a case number like `EDM-2026-0004` on its own.

## Reading the results

Results appear in up to three groups: **Clients**, **Cases** and **Tasks**. Each group shows at
most three results, and a group is hidden entirely if there's nothing to show.

| Group | What you see | What you can do |
|---|---|---|
| Clients | Client name, with their date of birth underneath | **Go to this client** opens their record |
| Cases | Case title, with the client's name and date of birth underneath | **Go to this case** opens the case |
| Tasks | Task title, with the case number and case title underneath | **View task** opens the task in a pop-up |

Tasks are ordered by how close their due date is to today, whether that date is in the past or the
future — so the most immediately relevant work comes first.

## Opening a task from search

Clicking **View task** opens the task in a pop-up window rather than navigating away, so you stay
on whatever screen you were working on. In the pop-up you can change the title, description and due
date, tick the task off as complete, delete it, or jump to its case.

Closing the pop-up leaves your search text and your results exactly as they were, so you can open
another result straight away.

## Privacy

Search runs entirely on your own device against your own data. Nothing you type into the search bar
is sent to an AI service or any other external system.
