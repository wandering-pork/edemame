# LMT Evidence and the Nomination Window

Labour Market Testing (LMT) evidence is required for employer-sponsored nominations. The
Department expects evidence of at least **two job advertisements**, each run for at least
**28 days**, and the nomination must be lodged **within 4 months of the advertising campaign's
closing date**. Miss that window and the evidence expires — the employer has to advertise the
position again before the nomination can be lodged.

Edamame tracks that window for you.

## Where to find it

Open the case and go to the **Workspace** tab. Under **Tools** you'll see **LMT Evidence** on cases
whose visa subclass carries an LMT requirement (482, 494 and 186). Click it to open the LMT
Evidence tab. It doesn't appear on subclasses that don't need LMT.

## Recording an advertisement

Click **Add advertisement**. You have two ways in:

1. **From the ad itself** — drop a screenshot or PDF of the job ad onto the upload area. Edamame
   reads the platform, the date the ad went live, and the closing date from the file.
2. **Manually** — click *Skip the scan and enter the dates manually* if you'd rather type them.

Either way you land on the same form, with the read values filled in. **Always check the dates
against the advertisement before saving** — the closing date is what sets your lodgement deadline,
so a misread date is a missed deadline. Edit anything that looks wrong, then click
**Confirm & add record**.

When you came in through a file, saving also adds that file to **Case Files** tagged with the
document type `LMTEVD — Labour Market Testing evidence`, and links it to the ad record so the
evidence and the dates always travel together.

Each record shows how many days the ad ran for, and flags any ad that ran for fewer than 28 days.
The panel also warns when fewer than two ads are recorded.

## The lodgement window

The **Nomination lodgement window** card at the top of the tab shows:

- **Campaign closed** — the latest closing date across all recorded ads.
- **Lodge nomination by** — that date plus 4 months.
- **Time remaining** — days left, or how long ago the window expired.

Re-running one ad extends the campaign, so adding a newer ad moves the deadline out automatically.

## The expiry alert

Once the window is within **30 days** of closing — or has already closed — and the nomination is
not yet recorded as lodged, a warning banner appears at the top of the case, an entry appears in
the case's **Alerts** list in the left rail, and a notification is raised in the bell menu.

If the window has expired, the banner's main action is **Start a new advertising round**, which
opens the LMT Evidence tab with the "add advertisement" form ready for the first ad of the new
campaign. Recording ads from the new campaign resets the deadline.

## How Edamame knows whether the nomination is lodged

The alert stands down as soon as the nomination is lodged. Edamame works that out from case data
you already keep:

- a **Document Checklist** item for the nomination (one using the `NOMAPP` document type, or whose
  name mentions "nomination") that has reached *Linked*, *Verified* or *Waived*; **or**
- a **completed task** whose title reads as lodging the nomination, e.g. "Lodge nomination".

If neither is true, the case counts as not lodged. The LMT Evidence tab always shows which of these
it matched, or why it didn't. If you've lodged and neither signal is set, click **Mark nomination as
lodged** (on the banner or in the tab) — it marks the nomination checklist item verified, adding a
"Nomination lodged" item to the checklist if the case doesn't have one. You can undo it at any time
by changing that checklist item's status back.

## What this feature is not

The 4-month rule as tracked here is the general rule. Real cases can have carve-outs — international
trade obligation exemptions, occupation-specific advertising requirements, and periods where the
Department has varied the requirements by legislative instrument. Treat the dates shown here as a
prompt to check the current policy for the case in front of you, not as legal advice.
